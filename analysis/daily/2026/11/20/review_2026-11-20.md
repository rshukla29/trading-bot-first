   Every other session's close line matches its opened quantity exactly (`2026-10-21`…`2026-11-19`: "2 contracts" for x2 opens, "1 contracts" for the 11-19 x1 open) — today's "4 contracts" for a position opened as `x2` is the first mismatch. The sequence leading up to it:
   ```

   2026-11-20 15:51:04,394 WARNING SPY: close filled 0 of 2 — 2 left, retrying
   2026-11-20 15:51:40,423 INFO SPY: long leg has no bid — buying back the short leg only (ask 0.01)
   2026-11-20 15:52:00,493 INFO SPY: CLOSED credit spread (close_1550), 4 contracts, cost 0.01, P&L 48.00
   ```
   `close_spread()`'s own comment (`run_credit_orb_bot.py:189-192`) documents the exact risk that materialized: "A cancelled order can still fill after the bot gave up on it." The first buy-to-close order was read as "0 of 2" filled and cancelled, but appears to have filled late; a second order for 2 more was fired before that late fill was recognized, so both filled — 4 bought against a short of 2. Two pieces of hard evidence confirm this, not just the log line: (a) tonight's **open positions** table shows `SPY 261120C00719000 OPT 2.00 @ 0.01` — the short leg itself showing up as an open long position, something no prior session's end-of-day table has shown (the short leg is supposed to net to flat); (b) `_finish_close`'s P&L formula, `(credit − cost) × filled × 100` with `filled=4`, gives exactly `(0.13−0.01)×4×100=$48.00` — double the `$24.00` the original 2-contract spread actually earned ((0.13−0.01)×2×100). Today's dollar impact is trivial (719C was trading near $0.01 by the close), but the same bug on a day when the short leg still has real value could mean paying for a real buy-back twice and booking a materially wrong P&L.
